    Keyboard & tips

    Type or paste a complete expression. Multiplication and division come before addition and subtraction. Parentheses work in all modes: 2*(3+4) or 2(3+4). In Standard and Scientific, select Fraction or Mixed below the result to show exact fractions and fraction-entry shortcuts.

    Use a decimal point or comma. Fractions: 1/2 + 3/4. Mixed numbers: 2 1/3 or -1 1/2. Scientific notation: 1e-6.

    % divides by 100: 200*10% = 20. To add 10%, use 200*(1+10%) = 220.

    Enter / = calculate · Esc clear · Backspace delete · F9 change sign.

    Ctrl/Cmd+C copies selected text, or the result when nothing is selected. Ctrl/Cmd+V pastes at the cursor. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes edits; Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redoes them.

    ↑ / ↓ recall expressions. A digit after = starts fresh; an operator continues with the exact result. Pressing = again keeps that result.

    ≈ marks a decimal rounded to 16 significant digits. Fraction and mixed views stay exact. Copy follows the selected result format. History is saved in this browser when storage is available.

    Scientific: functions use parentheses, e.g. sin(30), ln(e), sqrt(2). DEG/RAD also controls inverse trig. Powers associate right: 2^3^2 = 512; -2^2 = -4. Factorial requires an integer from 0 to 1000. Other functions include round, trunc and log10.

    Scientific approximations show ≈ and 15 significant digits; they stay approximate in history, memory and later calculations. Exact roots, integer powers and factorials keep full precision. Ans inserts the last calculated answer; memory buttons work in every mode.

    Programmer: unprefixed numbers use the selected base. Prefixes override it: 0xFF, 0b1010, 0o77, 0d255. Underscores can group digits. Changing base converts the expression without changing its value.

    Arithmetic wraps after each operation to 8, 16, 32 or 64 bits. Signed uses two's complement; integer division truncates toward zero. Operators: ~ (NOT), & (AND), | (OR), ^ (XOR), <<, >> and >>> (logical shift). Shift counts must be between zero and the word size. Typed aliases AND OR XOR NOT MOD SHL SHR USHR also work.

    Math and programmer modes keep separate drafts. History entries remember their angle unit, base and word settings. Click a base conversion to copy it with an explicit prefix.
